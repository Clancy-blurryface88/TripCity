## Design Context

### Users
Israeli travelers (Hebrew, RTL) planning a trip abroad — booking flights/hotels/car
rental/attractions themselves and using this app to hold it all in one place and build
a day-by-day itinerary. Used mostly on mobile, in short bursts (checking a booking,
glancing at tomorrow's plan), sometimes while actually traveling.

### Brand Personality
"Modern + Premium + Playful" (client's own words) — explicitly **not** a boring business
app. The destination city itself is the emotional hook: opening the app should feel like
looking at a little world you're about to visit, not opening a spreadsheet.

### Aesthetic Direction
- The city is the Home screen and the visual Hero — a charming, softly-lit isometric
  illustration of the destination (e.g. Paris with the Eiffel Tower), not a literal map.
- Reference: client-provided phone mockups (isometric island city, rounded pastel
  buildings, a river, small boat, a landmark centered and moderately sized — NOT
  dominating the whole screen), with small circular colored category markers pinned on
  top (flights=blue, hotels=purple, transport=green, car rental=orange,
  attractions=red, insurance=teal, checklist=violet). Markers are small and subtle,
  never oversized labels.
- Rounded cards, soft/diffuse shadows (not hard drop-shadows), generous white space,
  clean rounded sans typography, gentle spring/scale micro-animations — no bounce/elastic.
- Light theme only for MVP (bright daytime sky, soft pastel palette) — matches the
  "travel/vacation" mood; this is a deliberate choice, not a default.
- Anti-reference: generic "enterprise SaaS dashboard" look, boxy corporate cards, harsh
  shadows, oversized flat map pin icons.

### Design Principles
1. The illustration is the star — UI chrome (top bar, toggle, nav, FAB) floats on top of
   it as translucent/soft elements, never boxes it in with a hard card border.
2. Landmark scale discipline: the landmark is one recognizable element within a full
   little "island" scene (buildings + park + river + water), not the entire scene.
3. Category color-coding is consistent everywhere: same 7 colors/icons on the map
   markers, the bottom sheets, and the itinerary timeline.
4. Every interactive layer (bottom sheet, toggle, markers) uses spring motion, rounded
   corners, and soft shadows — never sharp corners or hard shadows.
5. RTL Hebrew first-class: text, icons, and layout direction all mirror correctly.
